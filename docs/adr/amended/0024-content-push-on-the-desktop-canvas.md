### ADR-0024: Content push on the desktop canvas

Date: 23 September 2026. Status: accepted (v0.7, milestone 0.7.17 stage 3); decision 2 amended 28 September, 3 October and 11 October 2026, earlier text in [amended/](0024-content-push-on-the-desktop-canvas.md#until-2026-10-11); decision 6 amended 14 October 2026 (three fit choices), earlier text in [amended/](0024-content-push-on-the-desktop-canvas.md#until-2026-10-14); decision 4 amended 31 October 2026 (deleted words give the growth back), earlier text in [amended/](0024-content-push-on-the-desktop-canvas.md#until-2026-10-31); decision 1 amended 10 November 2026 (a push moves only the blocks under the grown one, not those beside it), earlier text in [amended/](0024-content-push-on-the-desktop-canvas.md#until-2026-11-10); decision 6 amended 13 November 2026 (the shrink on the phone), earlier text in [amended/](0024-content-push-on-the-desktop-canvas.md#until-2026-11-13). How the pass is built is described in [ARCHITECTURE.md](../../ARCHITECTURE.md#desktop-sizing-the-push-pass-the-fit-and-the-gestures).

#### Context

Desktop blocks are placed absolutely from `frames.desktop`: `x`/`w` in percent of the content surface, `y`/`h` in pixels (ADR-0018). An absolutely positioned element is out of flow and reserves no space, so when a block's content becomes taller than its frame, the block grows its own box and draws over the blocks beneath. The content grows for four reasons: the window is narrower than the design width and the text wraps to more lines, a font loads or the visitor's font size differs, the text is longer in another language (ADR-0012), or a feed delivers more entries. ADR-0018 named this the content-driven half of the overflow and deferred it; a proportional scaling of the sections shrinks the picture between the binding width and a floor but does not make content push, and a hand-off to the mobile row grid at the binding width replaces the desktop layout with the phone layout on every tablet and small laptop window.

The survey of 23 September 2026 ([SURVEYS.md](../../comparison/SURVEYS.md#content-that-grows-must-push-23-september-2026)) found two proven models. Squarespace Fluid Engine stores blocks as grid coordinates on a CSS grid whose rows are `minmax(row-height, auto)`, so a row grows with its content and pushes the rows below in pure CSS; the editor removes the row stretch at drag start. The Wix Editor keeps absolute positions and publishes a push algorithm: only vertical position and size count, a gap of 70 px or less is preserved (the lower element moves the whole growth), a larger gap stays until it would fall under 10 px, an element whose top is above the grown element's vertical middle is a deliberate overlap and stays, containers expand from the bottom, and a move cascades to the elements below. Stacks and auto layout (Wix Studio, Framer, Figma) give up free placement.

Urd already uses the Fluid Engine model on mobile (ADR-0019). Porting it to the desktop was inventoried: about twenty files and ten test files to rewrite, and several invariants that break (sticky parking, the absolute canvas of ADR-0018, negative `y`, non-clipping sections, unaligned preset frames, `mobileOrder` keyed on desktop `y`); the list is in ARCHITECTURE.md.

#### Decision

1. **The push model, with the Wix Editor's published rules, applied to the blocks below the grown one.** After the blocks of a desktop section are rendered and have measured themselves, the engine measures each block's needed height (overflowing text counts) against `frames.desktop.h`, and the pure function `pushLayout` in `push-model.js` computes a shift per block: blocks are taken in ascending `y`; for a block whose growth (plus its own shift) is positive, every block whose top is at or below its vertical middle moves by the growth when the vertical gap is 70 px or less, and by the part of the growth that would bring the gap under 10 px otherwise; a block keeps the largest shift it receives, and its shift is growth for the blocks below it. Only a block that shares some of the grown block's width moves: the growth runs down the grown block's own column, so a block beside it, in another column of the section, keeps its place even when it starts lower, and two columns grow apart rather than one pushing the other. A pushed block pushes in turn the blocks under its own width. Blocks whose content never grows (images, shapes, buttons) never push.

2. **Shifts live on the elements, never in the data, and so does the measured height.** `frames.desktop` keeps the design: the push pass writes the shift on the element, and a pass without growth writes the design positions back. The text block and the data blocks never write their measured height into the frame; the editor accepts `urd-grow` from plugin copies (the compatibility surface). The frame's `h` changes only through an edit, and an edit of a block whose height follows its content is such an edit: the editor then asks the preview for the content's height at the design width and writes it into the frame (ADR-0025 decision 5); a measurement on the published page is never written anywhere. A section with a height of its own is the owner's: in the editor nothing raises it, and a block whose box lies past its edge counts for nothing in it and lies over the section below; on the published page the pass raises the section when the pushed content needs more, counting only the blocks whose design bottom is inside it, and returns to the design height when the growth is gone. A section with no height of its own follows every block.

3. **The pass runs for visitors and re-runs on change.** Urd renders the page in JavaScript, so Squarespace's reason for a CSS-only layout (a visitor site without repositioning scripts) does not apply; the pass runs after the blocks' own nets and re-runs when a block or its content changes size (fonts, images, feeds, width changes). On mobile the row grid handles growth and the pass does nothing.

4. **Typing is an edit of the design, and pushes in the data, both ways.** When the owner types more than the box holds, the text block grows its frame and moves the blocks below by the same rules, posting their new `y` in the same undo step, which is what the Wix Editor does in its editor. Words deleted give the room back: the frames are taken from where they stood before the typing grew them, so the text returns towards the height it had then, never shorter than its words, and the blocks below return with it. The start holds while nothing but the typing has changed the section's frames; a height the text had before the typing is the owner's. The editor otherwise drags on what is shown, with the push standing still, rather than returning every block to its design position at drag start as the Fluid Engine does: while a drag is in progress the push pass touches nothing, the frame the drag writes never contains the shift, and the push runs again from the new frames on release. Smart guides and the marquee align what the owner sees. A block dragged past a grown text can get a small settle at release, since its shift changes there; the page at rest is the same in the preview and on the published page, because the same pass draws both.

5. **No page zoom.** A page that only gets smaller adapts nothing, so the sections are never scaled proportionally between the binding width and a floor. Below the binding width the canvas is fluid, the push keeps the blocks apart, and each block decides for itself whether its content wraps or shrinks (decision 6). The mobile breakpoint is the site's own `breakpoints.mobile` at every width, and the menu folds only when its items no longer fit or at that breakpoint.

6. **What a block does below the design width is a choice per block, of three.** Any block whose content can wrap carries `fit` with `fitMin` (block-level fields, 0.01-1, default 0.6). Without the field the content wraps and pushes. `fit: "shrink"` zooms the content in proportion to the canvas (the canvas width over the design width), so its lines stay exactly as at the design width, down to the floor; under the floor the box is narrower than the content and it wraps. `fit: "both"` zooms the content only as much as the design height needs and lets it wrap, and past the floor it wraps and pushes like any other. A shrink never wraps and a wrap never shrinks; the choice that does both is named for it. The whole content is zoomed, so inline sizes follow. Where nothing has a design width (a full-width site or section) the proportional shrink has nothing to follow, so the editor offers wrap and «both» only. Image, video, shape and icon cannot wrap, so for them the same fields are a floor on the frame's width: the frame follows the canvas in percent as always but never gets narrower than the floor times its design width. On the phone (the row grid, [ADR-0019](../0019-synced-mobile-model.md)) «shrink» keeps the lines the same way, its content zoomed by the block's width there over its width at the design width, down to the floor; «both» wraps there, since the row grid has no design height, and a calendar draws its own phone view.

#### Consequences

- Content-driven overflow is closed: at the design width itself (fonts, zoom, languages, feeds), which ADR-0018 left open, and at every narrower width where text wraps to more lines.
- A section's height on the published page can differ from the editor's design height when the content grew; the difference is the pushed content, and it disappears when the content fits again. The editor shows the design height, the overlap included. The height drag and «fit height» keep working on the design height.
- The push pass owns the box height: a box grows with its content and comes back to the design when the content fits again, and is never set below the design height.
- Decision 6 gives both models the field uses: the proportional shrink is Wix Studio's «Scale proportionally» and Squarespace's scaled text, where the design is kept and only its size changes; «both» is the Framer «Fit Text» model, where nothing changes until the content no longer fits, so a block with room to spare keeps its size. A single choice that did both was found in testing to read as a wrap with smaller text, so the two are kept apart. Since the zoom sits on the block's content and never on the page, the editor's drags and the sticky logic work in plain px. Wix Studio (Scale proportionally with Max-min), Squarespace 7.1 (Site Styles minimum size) and Framer (Fit Text) are the precedents; the CSS `text-fit` property (Chrome 150 only) is the native future and stays on the watch list.
- Deliberate overlaps survive through the middle rule (a badge on an image, a title over a shape), but a block deliberately placed just below a text block within 70 px moves with the text; that is the intended reading and matches Wix.
- The desktop row grid remains the alternative that would also delete the measurement code; the inventory in the context is the price, and the decision is reopened only if the push model proves insufficient in the test rounds. A per-block «keep in place» flag (Wix's Fixed) stays deferred until a real page needs it.

### ADR-0024: Content push on the desktop canvas

Date: 23 September 2026. Status: accepted (v0.7, milestone 0.7.17 stage 3); decision 2 amended 28 September, 3 October and 11 October 2026, earlier text in [amended/](0024-content-push-on-the-desktop-canvas.md#until-2026-10-11); decision 6 amended 14 October 2026 (three fit choices), earlier text in [amended/](0024-content-push-on-the-desktop-canvas.md#until-2026-10-14). How the pass is built is described in [ARCHITECTURE.md](../../ARCHITECTURE.md#desktop-sizing-the-push-pass-the-fit-and-the-gestures).

#### Context

Desktop blocks are placed absolutely from `frames.desktop`: `x`/`w` in percent of the content surface, `y`/`h` in pixels (ADR-0018). An absolutely positioned element is out of flow and reserves no space, so when a block's content becomes taller than its frame, the block grows its own box and draws over the blocks beneath. The content grows for four reasons: the window is narrower than the design width and the text wraps to more lines, a font loads or the visitor's font size differs, the text is longer in another language (ADR-0012), or a feed delivers more entries. ADR-0018 named this the content-driven half of the overflow and deferred it; a proportional scaling of the sections shrinks the picture between the binding width and a floor but does not make content push, and a hand-off to the mobile row grid at the binding width replaces the desktop layout with the phone layout on every tablet and small laptop window.

The survey of 23 September 2026 ([SURVEYS.md](../../comparison/SURVEYS.md#content-that-grows-must-push-23-september-2026)) found two proven models. Squarespace Fluid Engine stores blocks as grid coordinates on a CSS grid whose rows are `minmax(row-height, auto)`, so a row grows with its content and pushes the rows below in pure CSS; the editor removes the row stretch at drag start. The Wix Editor keeps absolute positions and publishes a push algorithm: only vertical position and size count, a gap of 70 px or less is preserved (the lower element moves the whole growth), a larger gap stays until it would fall under 10 px, an element whose top is above the grown element's vertical middle is a deliberate overlap and stays, containers expand from the bottom, and a move cascades to the elements below. Stacks and auto layout (Wix Studio, Framer, Figma) give up free placement.

Urd already uses the Fluid Engine model on mobile (ADR-0019). Porting it to the desktop was inventoried: about twenty files and ten test files to rewrite, and several invariants that break (sticky parking, the absolute canvas of ADR-0018, negative `y`, non-clipping sections, unaligned preset frames, `mobileOrder` keyed on desktop `y`); the list is in ARCHITECTURE.md.

#### Decision

1. **The push model, with the Wix Editor's published rules, applied to every block below.** After the blocks of a desktop section are rendered and have measured themselves, the engine measures each block's needed height (overflowing text counts) against `frames.desktop.h`, and the pure function `pushLayout` in `push-model.js` computes a shift per block: blocks are taken in ascending `y`; for a block whose growth (plus its own shift) is positive, every block whose top is at or below its vertical middle moves by the growth when the vertical gap is 70 px or less, and by the part of the growth that would bring the gap under 10 px otherwise; a block keeps the largest shift it receives, and its shift is growth for the blocks below it. Horizontal position is ignored, as at Wix and Squarespace: a block beside the grown one but starting lower moves too, which is the predictable reading of «everything below moves». Blocks whose content never grows (images, shapes, buttons) never push.

2. **Shifts live on the elements, never in the data, and so does the measured height.** `frames.desktop` keeps the design: the push pass writes the shift on the element, and a pass without growth writes the design positions back. The text block and the data blocks never write their measured height into the frame; the editor accepts `urd-grow` from plugin copies (the compatibility surface). The frame's `h` changes only through an edit, and an edit of a block whose height follows its content is such an edit: the editor then asks the preview for the content's height at the design width and writes it into the frame (ADR-0025 decision 5); a measurement on the published page is never written anywhere. A section with a height of its own is the owner's: in the editor nothing raises it, and a block whose box lies past its edge counts for nothing in it and lies over the section below; on the published page the pass raises the section when the pushed content needs more, counting only the blocks whose design bottom is inside it, and returns to the design height when the growth is gone. A section with no height of its own follows every block.

3. **The pass runs for visitors and re-runs on change.** Urd renders the page in JavaScript, so Squarespace's reason for a CSS-only layout (a visitor site without repositioning scripts) does not apply; the pass runs after the blocks' own nets and re-runs when a block or its content changes size (fonts, images, feeds, width changes). On mobile the row grid handles growth and the pass does nothing.

4. **Typing is an edit of the design, and pushes in the data.** When the owner types more than the box holds, the text block grows its frame and moves the blocks below by the same rules, posting their new `y` in the same undo step, which is what the Wix Editor does in its editor. The editor otherwise drags on what is shown, with the push standing still, rather than returning every block to its design position at drag start as the Fluid Engine does: while a drag is in progress the push pass touches nothing, the frame the drag writes never contains the shift, and the push runs again from the new frames on release. Smart guides and the marquee align what the owner sees. A block dragged past a grown text can get a small settle at release, since its shift changes there; the page at rest is the same in the preview and on the published page, because the same pass draws both.

5. **No page zoom.** A page that only gets smaller adapts nothing, so the sections are never scaled proportionally between the binding width and a floor. Below the binding width the canvas is fluid, the push keeps the blocks apart, and each block decides for itself whether its content wraps or shrinks (decision 6). The mobile breakpoint is the site's own `breakpoints.mobile` at every width, and the menu folds only when its items no longer fit or at that breakpoint.

6. **What a block does below the design width is a choice per block, of three.** Any block whose content can wrap carries `fit` with `fitMin` (block-level fields, 0.01-1, default 0.6). Without the field the content wraps and pushes. `fit: "shrink"` zooms the content in proportion to the canvas (the canvas width over the design width), so its lines stay exactly as at the design width, down to the floor; under the floor the box is narrower than the content and it wraps. `fit: "both"` zooms the content only as much as the design height needs and lets it wrap, and past the floor it wraps and pushes like any other. A shrink never wraps and a wrap never shrinks; the choice that does both is named for it. The whole content is zoomed, so inline sizes follow. Where nothing has a design width (a full-width site or section) the proportional shrink has nothing to follow, so the editor offers wrap and «both» only. Image, video, shape and icon cannot wrap, so for them the same fields are a floor on the frame's width: the frame follows the canvas in percent as always but never gets narrower than the floor times its design width.

#### Consequences

- Content-driven overflow is closed: at the design width itself (fonts, zoom, languages, feeds), which ADR-0018 left open, and at every narrower width where text wraps to more lines.
- A section's height on the published page can differ from the editor's design height when the content grew; the difference is the pushed content, and it disappears when the content fits again. The editor shows the design height, the overlap included. The height drag and «fit height» keep working on the design height.
- The push pass owns the box height: a box grows with its content and comes back to the design when the content fits again, and is never set below the design height.
- Decision 6 gives both models the field uses: the proportional shrink is Wix Studio's «Scale proportionally» and Squarespace's scaled text, where the design is kept and only its size changes; «both» is the Framer «Fit Text» model, where nothing changes until the content no longer fits, so a block with room to spare keeps its size. A single choice that did both was found in testing to read as a wrap with smaller text, so the two are kept apart. Desktop only; the row grid wraps naturally. Since the zoom sits on the block's content and never on the page, the editor's drags and the sticky logic work in plain px. Wix Studio (Scale proportionally with Max-min), Squarespace 7.1 (Site Styles minimum size) and Framer (Fit Text) are the precedents; the CSS `text-fit` property (Chrome 150 only) is the native future and stays on the watch list.
- Deliberate overlaps survive through the middle rule (a badge on an image, a title over a shape), but a block deliberately placed just below a text block within 70 px moves with the text; that is the intended reading and matches Wix.
- The desktop row grid remains the alternative that would also delete the measurement code; the inventory in the context is the price, and the decision is reopened only if the push model proves insufficient in the test rounds. A per-block «keep in place» flag (Wix's Fixed) stays deferred until a real page needs it.

#### Addendum, 28 September 2026: a block past the section's height has no say in it

Decision 2 raised the section to `bottom + 24` whenever anything in it grew, with `bottom` measured over every block. A block deliberately placed across the boundary was therefore dragged into that maximum, and the section stretched to keep it inside instead of letting it lie over the section below. The rule is narrowed: a block counts towards the section's height only while its design bottom (`y + h`) is inside the section's own height. Content that grows within that height still pushes the blocks under it and still raises the section; a block hung past the edge is left out of the calculation, and its section keeps the height the owner set. A section with no height of its own still follows every block, since there is nothing to measure against.

#### Addendum, 3 October 2026: the editor may fit a frame after an edit

Decision 2 keeps measured heights out of `frames.desktop`. ADR-0025 amends it in one respect: when the owner edits a block whose height follows its content (a design, a variant, a setting, its width), the editor asks the preview for the height the content needs at the design width and writes it into the frame, since an edit of the block is an edit of the design. A measurement taken on the published page is still never written anywhere, and the push pass covers the difference as before.
