# ADR-0024: Content push on the desktop canvas

Date: 23 September 2026. Status: accepted (v0.7, milestone 0.7.17 stage 3); decision 2 amended 28 September, 3 October and 11 October 2026, earlier text in [amended/](amended/0024-content-push-on-the-desktop-canvas-until-2026-10-11.md). How the pass is built is described in [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md#desktop-sizing-the-push-pass-the-fit-and-the-gestures).

## Context

Desktop blocks are placed absolutely from `frames.desktop`: `x`/`w` in percent of the content surface, `y`/`h` in pixels (ADR-0018). An absolutely positioned element is out of flow and reserves no space, so when a block's content becomes taller than its frame, the block grows its own box and draws over the blocks beneath. The content grows for four reasons: the window is narrower than the design width and the text wraps to more lines, a font loads or the visitor's font size differs, the text is longer in another language (ADR-0012), or a feed delivers more entries. ADR-0018 named this the content-driven half of the overflow and deferred it; a proportional scaling of the sections shrinks the picture between the binding width and a floor but does not make content push, and a hand-off to the mobile row grid at the binding width replaces the desktop layout with the phone layout on every tablet and small laptop window.

The survey of 23 September 2026 (LAERDOMMER §2) found two proven models. Squarespace Fluid Engine stores blocks as grid coordinates on a CSS grid whose rows are `minmax(row-height, auto)`, so a row grows with its content and pushes the rows below in pure CSS; the editor removes the row stretch at drag start. The Wix Editor keeps absolute positions and publishes a push algorithm: only vertical position and size count, a gap of 70 px or less is preserved (the lower element moves the whole growth), a larger gap stays until it would fall under 10 px, an element whose top is above the grown element's vertical middle is a deliberate overlap and stays, containers expand from the bottom, and a move cascades to the elements below. Stacks and auto layout (Wix Studio, Framer, Figma) give up free placement.

Urd already uses the Fluid Engine model on mobile (ADR-0019). Porting it to the desktop was inventoried: about twenty files and ten test files to rewrite, and several invariants that break (sticky parking, the absolute canvas of ADR-0018, negative `y`, non-clipping sections, unaligned preset frames, `mobileOrder` keyed on desktop `y`); the list is in ARCHITECTURE.md.

## Decision

1. **The push model, with the Wix Editor's published rules, applied to every block below.** After the blocks of a desktop section are rendered and have measured themselves, the engine measures each block's needed height (overflowing text counts) against `frames.desktop.h`, and the pure function `pushLayout` in `push-model.js` computes a shift per block: blocks are taken in ascending `y`; for a block whose growth (plus its own shift) is positive, every block whose top is at or below its vertical middle moves by the growth when the vertical gap is 70 px or less, and by the part of the growth that would bring the gap under 10 px otherwise; a block keeps the largest shift it receives, and its shift is growth for the blocks below it. Horizontal position is ignored, as at Wix and Squarespace: a block beside the grown one but starting lower moves too, which is the predictable reading of «everything below moves». Blocks whose content never grows (images, shapes, buttons) never push.

2. **Shifts live on the elements, never in the data, and so does the measured height.** `frames.desktop` keeps the design: the push pass writes the shift on the element, and a pass without growth writes the design positions back. The text block and the data blocks never write their measured height into the frame; the editor accepts `urd-grow` from plugin copies (the compatibility surface). The frame's `h` changes only through an edit, and an edit of a block whose height follows its content is such an edit: the editor then asks the preview for the content's height at the design width and writes it into the frame (ADR-0025 decision 5); a measurement on the published page is never written anywhere. A section with a height of its own is the owner's: in the editor nothing raises it, and a block whose box lies past its edge counts for nothing in it and lies over the section below; on the published page the pass raises the section when the pushed content needs more, counting only the blocks whose design bottom is inside it, and returns to the design height when the growth is gone. A section with no height of its own follows every block.

3. **The pass runs for visitors and re-runs on change.** Urd renders the page in JavaScript, so Squarespace's reason for a CSS-only layout (a visitor site without repositioning scripts) does not apply; the pass runs after the blocks' own nets and re-runs when a block or its content changes size (fonts, images, feeds, width changes). On mobile the row grid handles growth and the pass does nothing.

4. **Typing is an edit of the design, and pushes in the data.** When the owner types more than the box holds, the text block grows its frame and moves the blocks below by the same rules, posting their new `y` in the same undo step, which is what the Wix Editor does in its editor. The editor otherwise drags on what is shown, with the push standing still, rather than returning every block to its design position at drag start as the Fluid Engine does: while a drag is in progress the push pass touches nothing, the frame the drag writes never contains the shift, and the push runs again from the new frames on release. Smart guides and the marquee align what the owner sees. A block dragged past a grown text can get a small settle at release, since its shift changes there; the page at rest is the same in the preview and on the published page, because the same pass draws both.

5. **No page zoom.** A page that only gets smaller adapts nothing, so the sections are never scaled proportionally between the binding width and a floor. Below the binding width the canvas is fluid, the push keeps the blocks apart, and each block decides for itself whether its content wraps or shrinks (decision 6). The mobile breakpoint is the site's own `breakpoints.mobile` at every width, and the menu folds only when its items no longer fit or at that breakpoint.

6. **Shrink instead of wrap is a choice per block.** Any block whose content can wrap can carry `fit: "shrink"` with `fitMin` (block-level fields, 0.01-1, default 0.6). The content keeps its size as long as it fits the design height; when the frame is too narrow, the push pass zooms the content only as much as the design height needs, and past the floor the content wraps and pushes like any other. The whole content is zoomed, so inline sizes follow. Image, video, shape and icon cannot wrap, so for them the same fields are a floor on the frame's width: the frame follows the canvas in percent as always but never gets narrower than the floor times its design width.

## Consequences

- Content-driven overflow is closed: at the design width itself (fonts, zoom, languages, feeds), which ADR-0018 left open, and at every narrower width where text wraps to more lines.
- A section's height on the published page can differ from the editor's design height when the content grew; the difference is the pushed content, and it disappears when the content fits again. The editor shows the design height, the overlap included. The height drag and «fit height» keep working on the design height.
- The push pass owns the box height: a box grows with its content and comes back to the design when the content fits again, and is never set below the design height.
- The shrink of decision 6 is the Framer «Fit Text» model rather than ApeironLF's continuous `clamp()`: nothing changes until the content no longer fits, so a block with room to spare keeps its size. Desktop only; the row grid wraps naturally. Since the zoom sits on the block's content and never on the page, the editor's drags and the sticky logic work in plain px. Wix Studio (Scale proportionally with Max-min), Squarespace 7.1 (Site Styles minimum size) and Framer (Fit Text) offer the same choice; the CSS `text-fit` property (Chrome 150 only) is the native future and stays on the watch list.
- Deliberate overlaps survive through the middle rule (a badge on an image, a title over a shape), but a block deliberately placed just below a text block within 70 px moves with the text; that is the intended reading and matches Wix.
- The desktop row grid remains the alternative that would also delete the measurement code; the inventory in the context is the price, and the decision is reopened only if the push model proves insufficient in the test rounds. A per-block «keep in place» flag (Wix's Fixed) stays deferred until a real page needs it.
