# ADR-0024: Content push on the desktop canvas

Date: 23 September 2026. Status: accepted (v0.7, milestone 0.7.17 stage 3).

## Context

Desktop blocks are placed absolutely from `frames.desktop`: `x`/`w` in percent of the content surface, `y`/`h` in pixels (ADR-0018). An absolutely positioned element is out of flow and reserves no space, so when a block's content becomes taller than its frame, the block grows its own box (the render-time net in blocks/text.js and the ten data blocks) and draws over the blocks beneath. The content grows for four reasons: the window is narrower than the design width and the text wraps to more lines, a font loads or the visitor's font size differs, the text is longer in another language (ADR-0012), or a feed delivers more entries. ADR-0018 named this the content-driven half of the overflow and deferred it; the scaling of the ADR-0018 addendum of 23 September 2026 shrinks the picture between the binding width and a floor but does not make content push, and its hand-off to the mobile row grid at 1227 px replaced the desktop layout with the phone layout on every tablet and small laptop window.

The survey of 23 September 2026 (LAERDOMMER §2) found two proven models. Squarespace Fluid Engine stores blocks as grid coordinates on a CSS grid whose rows are `minmax(row-height, auto)`, so a row grows with its content and pushes the rows below in pure CSS; the editor removes the row stretch at drag start. The Wix Editor keeps absolute positions and publishes a push algorithm: only vertical position and size count, a gap of 70 px or less is preserved (the lower element moves the whole growth), a larger gap stays until it would fall under 10 px, an element whose top is above the grown element's vertical middle is a deliberate overlap and stays, containers expand from the bottom, and a move cascades to the elements below. Stacks and auto layout (Wix Studio, Framer, Figma) give up free placement.

Urd already uses the Fluid Engine model on mobile (ADR-0019). Porting it to the desktop was inventoried: about twenty files rewritten (frameToCss and the desktop branch of render.js, base.css, sticky.js, twelve sites in preview-edit.js, place.js, section-size.js, selection.js, section-layouts.js, the 112 hand-written frames in sections/presets.js, templates-model.js, preset-thumb.js, migrate.js) and ten test files; and several invariants break: sticky.js reads a block's `y` from `style.top` and writes it back when parked, `.urd-canvas` must leave `position: absolute` (ADR-0018 decision 2), `y` may be negative for blocks hanging above the section, sections never clip while a grid reserves space for every item, 100 of the 112 preset `y` and `h` values are not aligned to a row step, `mobileOrder` and the presets' card order use desktop `y` as a pixel-scale key, and a row step would have to be a model constant rather than `grid.size`.

## Decision

1. **The push model, with the Wix Editor's published rules, applied to every block below.** After the blocks of a desktop section are rendered and have measured themselves, the engine measures each block's needed height (`max(offsetHeight, scrollHeight)`, so overflowing text counts) against `frames.desktop.h`, and the pure function `pushLayout` in `push-model.js` computes a shift per block: blocks are taken in ascending `y`; for a block whose growth (plus its own shift) is positive, every block whose top is at or below its vertical middle moves by the growth when the vertical gap is `PUSH_GAP_MAX` (70 px) or less, and by the part of the growth that would bring the gap under `PUSH_GAP_MIN` (10 px) otherwise; a block keeps the largest shift it receives, and its shift is growth for the blocks below it. Horizontal position is ignored, as at Wix and Squarespace: a block beside the grown one but starting lower moves too, which is the predictable reading of «everything below moves». Blocks whose content never grows (images, shapes, buttons) never push.

2. **Shifts live on the elements, never in the data, and so does the measured height.** `frames.desktop` keeps the design: `frameToCss` writes the design position, the push pass writes `style.top` and `data-urd-shift`, and a pass without growth writes the design positions back. The text block and the ten data blocks no longer write their measured height into the frame in the preview (the `urd-grow` amendment of ADR-0018): with the frame equal to the measurement there was no growth left for the push to see, and the neighbours stayed put in the editor while they moved on the published page. The editor still accepts `urd-grow` from plugin copies (the compatibility surface), and the frame's `h` changes only through an edit. The section grows to `bottom + 24` when the pushed content needs more than its design height (`data-urd-min-height` keeps the design value) and returns to it when the growth is gone. A block pinned by sticky.js keeps its stash up to date instead of its inline top, so a release lands right.

3. **The pass runs for visitors and re-runs on change.** Urd renders the page in JavaScript, so Squarespace's reason for a CSS-only layout (a visitor site without repositioning scripts) does not apply; the pass is one `requestAnimationFrame` after the blocks' own nets and a `ResizeObserver` on every block and its content (fonts, images, feeds, width changes), deduplicated to one pass per frame per section. On mobile the row grid handles growth and the pass does nothing.

4. **Typing is an edit of the design, and pushes in the data.** When the owner types more than the box holds, the text block grows its frame (as before) and now also moves the blocks below by the same rules, posting their new `y` in the same undo step, which is what the Wix Editor does in its editor. The editor otherwise drags on the design, not on the push: `suspendPush`/`resumePush` sit beside `suspendSticky`/`resumeSticky` in preview-edit.js: at drag, resize, arrow-key, align and group-drag start every block returns to its design position, and the push runs again on release. Smart guides and the marquee measure `offsetTop`, which is the design position while suspended. This is the Fluid Engine's «remove the row stretch at drag start».

5. **The scaling band keeps its floor and loses its hand-off.** Between the binding width and the floor the sections zoom (ADR-0018 addendum); below the floor the factor stays at the floor, the canvas is fluid again and the push keeps the blocks apart. The mobile breakpoint is the site's own `breakpoints.mobile` at every width, and the menu folds only when its items no longer fit or at that breakpoint.

## Consequences

- The overlap of bilde 6 (a wrapped paragraph over a button) is gone at every width, and so is the content-driven overflow at the design width itself (fonts, zoom, languages, feeds), which ADR-0018 left open.
- A section's height on the published page can differ from the editor's design height when the content grew; the difference is the pushed content, and it disappears when the content fits again. The height drag and «fit height» keep working on the design height.
- The push pass owns the box height: it measures the content children with the box at the design height (the editing handles never count, a zoomed child is converted to the block's px) and sets the box to the content, never below the design height. A box therefore grows with its content and comes back to the design when the content fits again, which the one-way nets of ADR-0018 could not do.
- **Shrink instead of wrap** (23 September 2026, the ApeironLF heading model): a text block can carry `fit: "shrink"` with `fitMin` (0.01-1, default 0.6; the whole range is the owner's). The content keeps its size as long as it fits the design height, so nothing changes until the frame is too narrow for the text at full size; then the push pass zooms the content only as much as the design height needs (a bisection between full size and the floor, measured before the box height), never under the floor (a share of the design size, so the section zoom is divided out of it), and past the floor it wraps and pushes like any other; the whole content is zoomed so inline sizes from the toolbar follow. Desktop only; the row grid wraps naturally. Wix Studio (Scale proportionally with Max-min), Squarespace 7.1 (Site Styles minimum size) and Framer (Fit Text) offer the same choice; the CSS `text-fit` property (Chrome 150 only) is the native future and stays on the watch list.
- Deliberate overlaps survive through the middle rule (a badge on an image, a title over a shape), but a block deliberately placed just below a text block within 70 px moves with the text; that is the intended reading and matches Wix.
- The desktop row grid remains the alternative that would also delete the measurement code; the inventory above is the price, and the decision is reopened only if the push model proves insufficient in the test rounds. A per-block «keep in place» flag (Wix's Fixed) stays deferred until a real page needs it.
