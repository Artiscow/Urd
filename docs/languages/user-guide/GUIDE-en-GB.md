## Getting started

1. Go to `/admin` on your site (for example `yoursite.pages.dev/admin`).
2. Log in with your GitHub account (the **Log in with GitHub** button in the top right). You can see and try the editor without logging in, but not publish.
3. Everything you change is saved as a **draft** in your browser straight away. Nothing becomes visible to visitors until you press **Publish**.

## The editor

The screen has three parts:

- **The top bar**: the open page's name, which lists the site's pages with a search to switch to one, each with a gear to its settings; undo and redo, greyed when there is nothing to take back, with an arrow that lists the session's steps to go back or forward to one (the steps are kept in this browser over a reload); the screen and phone buttons; and publish.
- **The panel rail on the left**: an icon for each panel, grouped by workflow and named in a tooltip: Pages, Blocks, Settings and Grid (building the page), Site, Legal, Theme, Nav, Footer, Collections and Plugins (the site), and History and Updates. Click to open a panel; click again, or the fold button in the panel's head, to close. Urd settings (the gear at the rail's foot) has «Panel navigation» with four other forms: a sidebar of words, tabs at the top of the panel, a strip of every panel under the top bar, or the page's panels as tabs with a Site menu in the top bar. In the strip, a panel with folds (Site, Legal, Theme, Nav and Footer) has a small arrow after its name (in the panel's own tabs too, with **Menus under the tabs** switched on in Urd settings): resting the mouse on the tab, or the down arrow key on it, opens a menu of its folds, and a row opens the panel with that fold alone unfolded. One menu stands open at a time: resting on the next tab closes the first at once. The page switcher at the top left and the device buttons' sizes open when the mouse rests on them, in every layout, and so does the Site menu of «The page's tabs»; and the same menu of folds opens beside its rows and beside an icon of the rail (headed there by the panel's name, which opens the panel, in place of the tooltip), on a rest or the right arrow key, and the left arrow goes back. The sidebar of words lists the open panel's folds under its word, and Collections its collections; «Folds in the sidebar» in Urd settings also shows them under the word on hover (**Under the word, on hover**), the open panel's staying open, or in the menu beside each word. Site's, Nav's and Footer's folds stand in short groups under small labels; «Tab menus» in Urd settings sets the rows apart with a faint line instead, or shows them as a plain list, and a menu stays open after a row is picked until the mouse leaves it, unless «After a pick» under it is set to close it at once. A row that does something at once carries that thing's mark: Footer's Design, with the four squares, opens the design picker. The Collections tab's menu lists the site's collections, a new one as soon as it is made; resting the mouse on a collection, or the right arrow key, opens its entries beside it, newest first (a product catalogue in its own order), and a click on an entry opens it in the Collections panel. Resting on an entry opens a third menu of its common actions: **Add image** (or **Edit image**, which opens the image editor on it) and **Duplicate**. **New entry** at the foot of a collection's entries adds one and opens it, and **New collection** at the menu's foot opens the Collections panel with the caret in the new collection's name. A dot on Updates says a new Urd is out; «Update notice» in Urd settings can make it a pill in the top bar instead, shared by everyone who edits the site.
- **Ctrl+K opens the command palette**: type a command, a page, a panel or a block and press Enter. Like the editor's other searches, it finds the editor's own words (commands, panels, blocks, settings, designs, icons) typed in another of Urd's languages or an installed language pack's too, whatever the admin and the site are set to: «innstillinger» finds Settings in an English admin. Your own words (page names, entries, files) are found as you wrote them. The pin on a row makes the command a shared shortcut, shown first in the palette for everyone who edits the site and published with it.
- **Admin theme**: «Colour theme» in Urd settings switches the editor's own colour theme (seven variants). It never affects your website.
- **Button press**: how the editor's buttons answer a press, in Urd settings: Ripple (the default), Press in, Nudge, Sink, Ring or Spring. With reduced motion on in your system, only the colour changes.
- **Moving rows**: a row in a list (a background layer, a link, a question, an entry) is moved by pressing, holding and dragging the row itself, or with Alt and the arrow keys. A card of the row follows the pointer, the row stays dimmed in its place, and a slot shows where it will land; in Urd settings, Moving rows can show a line between the rows instead. Escape cancels the move, and the row flashes once where it lands.
- **Urd settings**: the gear opens Urd's own settings as a dialog with the groups down the left and a search above them; every setting that changes a look is chosen from a picture of each choice, and «Look choices» turns the pictures to words. «Settings window» moves the settings into a panel in the panel column, so the page stays in view, or a sheet with the look at the left. «Panels on a fresh load» chooses what the admin opens with: the panel you last had, none, always the same panel, or in the tab layouts the column folded. The settings under «This site», marked Shared, are stored in the site for everyone who edits it; the rest are kept in your browser.
- **The preview**: your real page. What you see is what visitors get.

**Device** (top bar): a press on the device not shown switches to it, a press on the one shown lists its sizes. **Screen** is your own window, and its list holds the screen sizes (small, regular and large laptops and a full HD screen, each with its height, or your own size); **Phone** shows a real phone size with its height, and its list holds the sizes (small, wide and large phones, a tablet, the width alone, or your own size), remembered in this browser. Every size shows its aspect ratio, and under **Aspect ratios** a common ratio (16:9 or 21:9 for a screen, 19.5:9 or 20:9 for a phone) keeps the width you stand at and sets the height from the ratio. Drag the bar at the canvas's right edge to see the page at any width: the number follows the pointer and snaps to the devices and the site's phone breakpoint; press a device to leave it.

**Clean view** (top right) hides all the tools so you see the page completely without editor frames. Click the **Edit** button to come back.

## Editing content

- **Writing text**: click in a text block and type straight into the page.
- **Moving a block**: grab it and drag. The block snaps to the guide lines (see Grid below).
- **Phone design**: the gallery, collection, contact details, statistics, privacy page and terms of sale have a **Phone design** row under their Design: the design the block draws while it is narrow, on a phone or in a narrow column of a wide page, for example the long read on a computer and the questions on a phone. **Same as the design** keeps the block's own. In the phone view the toolbar's design button opens it, and the gear the block's menu.
- **Resizing**: drag the handle in the corner of the block. A block whose content decides its height (Calendar, Collection, Product cards, Basket, Checkout, Contact details, Countdown, FAQ, Form, Quote, Share buttons, Statistic, Table, Timeline and Audio) is always as tall as what it shows: its handle changes the width only, a pull up or down stops at the edge of the content with a mark that says so, and its outline follows by itself when you change its settings. A block with columns or cards (Calendar, Collection, Gallery, Product cards, Statistic, Timeline and Table) lays itself out by its own width: dragged narrower it shows its narrow layout at once, and in a narrow column of a wide page it looks as it does on a phone.
- **The toolbar above the selected block**:
  - ⠿ move (drag)
  - the layer arrows (arrow towards a line, up/down) put the block right in front or at the very back (when blocks overlap). NB: while you edit, the block you point at or have selected is always shown on top, so the handles can be reached - you see the REAL order in Clean view and on the published page
  - the phone icon: phone visibility (decor), see below
  - ⚙ the block's settings, in a floating menu beside it; at the foot of its Placement, duplicate, to the front, to the back and delete (the first press arms it, the second deletes)
  - × delete the block
- **Rotating**: drag the little ⟳ circle in the block's top right corner (snaps to 15° steps; hold Shift for a free angle). An exact number of degrees can also be set in Settings.
- **Keyboard on a selected block**: the arrow keys move one grid step (Shift = 1 px), Delete deletes, Esc deselects, Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V copy, cut and paste (a copy pastes on another page too), Ctrl+D duplicates, Ctrl+Alt+C and Ctrl+Alt+V copy and paste the block's style, Ctrl+Shift+↑ and ↓ put it in front or behind, Shift+F10 opens the block's right-click menu (a right click on the section's empty surface opens the section's). With a section selected and no block, Ctrl+C and Ctrl+X take the whole section, and Ctrl+V pastes a copied section below the selected one.
- **Keyboard in the block menu**: 1, 2 and 3 choose Content, Style and Placement, / goes to the search, Esc closes the floating menu.
- **Snap lines**: when you drag a block near the edge or centre of another block, a line appears between the two and the block snaps into place; at the content's quarters or its middle, a short line with its share appears beside the block; when its distance to a neighbour equals the distance between two neighbours, the distances are shown as bands with the number in them. The lines also come from the section you hold the block over. Hold Shift to move straight across or straight down, and Ctrl (Cmd on a Mac) for completely free placement without the grid and the lines.
- **Undo**: Ctrl+Z (and Ctrl+Shift+Z to redo) works on everything: moving, size, text, deletion, sections and grid.
- **All the keys in one list**: press **?** on the page or in the panels, or choose **Keyboard shortcuts** in the Urd settings (the gear at the bottom of the panel selector), for every key the editor answers to, grouped by where it works.

### Adding blocks

Open the **Blocks** panel and click the block you want; it is placed in the middle of the viewport, in the section you last clicked in. The types:

- **Text**: ordinary text straight on the page.
- **Text box**: text in a card with a background colour and rounded corners, nice for help texts and information boxes.
- **Button**: a link to another page or an external address.
- **Image**: upload from your machine. The image is compressed automatically.
- **Video**: paste a YouTube or Vimeo link in Settings. The embed is privacy-friendly, and the video plays on the published page (in the editor a click only selects the block). Nothing loads from YouTube or Vimeo until a visitor presses play: the video stands behind a cover in the site's colours, or your own picture (**Choose a cover image**, then **Edit cover image** or the pencil on the block for its focus point, zoom and adjustments), with a line saying where it loads from and a box to load from that provider at once on the site from then on. Maps, and the contact block's map, stand behind the same cover. **Ask before loading** (on by default, in the video's, the map's and the contact block's settings) can be switched off to load the video or map with the page and no cover; the provider then learns of every visit and may store data in the visitor's browser without asking, which the law asks consent for. **Shape** sets the video's proportions (16:9, 4:3, 1:1, 9:16 for vertical videos, 21:9 or 4:5) keeping the block's height and setting its width from it; the block then keeps that shape at every width, its height following as you drag its side handles, and **Start playing when pressed** (on by default) starts the video as soon as the cover is pressed.
- **Collection**: shows the entries from a collection (see the Collections panel) as cards, a list or a year-grouped archive.
- **Icon**: a character or an emoji in any size and theme colour. In Settings the character button opens a menu with hundreds of characters and emojis (with the recently used at the top), and at the bottom of the menu you can upload your own icon image that is shown instead of the character.
- **Shapes**: line, arrow, circle, rectangle and triangle for decoration.
- **Privacy page**: the site's privacy statement, written from the site itself: who is responsible (the contact details in the Legal panel), what each form and the shop send and to whom, whether visits are counted, which companies' videos and maps the pages hold, the host, the visitor's rights, and a list of the companies the visitor has let load with the page, each taken back with one press. What it says has no settings of its own: change the site and the page follows. A new page from the **Privacy** template holds it with its heading in the site's language (**Page heading** in its menu takes the heading away where the page has its own), and the footer then links it on every page (**Privacy page** under Privacy in the **Legal** panel chooses another, or none). **Design** in its menu's Appearance draws the page as a **Long read**, with the **Contents at the side**, with the main points **At a glance** as four cards, as **Questions** that fold out, or as a **Ledger table** or **Ledger cards** that set out what is collected, why, who gets it and how long it is kept; the colours, the lines over the parts, the corners, the fonts and **Placement** (centred in the block, or at its left edge) stand beside it.
- **Terms of sale**: the terms of sale as a block of their own, written on the page as a text is, and drawn in a document design under **Design** in its menu's Appearance: **Long read** (room between the paragraphs and a short line over each part), **Contents at the side** (the parts listed at the side of the text from its second-level headings, on the left or the right as **Contents side** says, the text **Centred** with the list at the block's outer edge (once the block is about 950 px wide) or beside the list, following the reader down, and folded into one row on a phone), **At a glance** (the first list before the first heading as cards, each item's bold words as its label), **Questions** (each part folds out under its heading, one at a time, for visitors; in the editor every part stands open so you can write in it) and **Numbered clauses** (each part and its paragraphs numbered, 7.1, 7.2). Beside the design: the accent, heading and card colours, the lines over the parts, the corners and the heading font, each shown only where the design draws it. The **Terms of sale** page template starts with one, written in the site's language with its facts, and the block list adds one with the same text to fill in. A text that stood in a document design before is a Terms of sale block after the update, its words and look kept.

In the editor, links and buttons are never triggered when you click them - the click selects the block. Test the links via **View the published site** in the Publish menu.

### What does the phone icon (decor) mean?

The button shows whether the block comes along to phones. On phones the content is stacked automatically in one column, and there decoration is skipped: a slanted line or arrow that sits nicely behind the text on desktop is just clutter in the middle of a phone column.

- Phone = the block is content and is shown on phones.
- Crossed-out phone (yellow button) = the block is decoration (decor) and is hidden in the automatic phone layout.

Click to switch. New shapes and icons from the Blocks panel start as decoration (icons from the section templates are content and are shown on phones). (If you have hand-tuned the phone layout in the section, you place everything yourself anyway; then the flag means nothing there.)

## The Settings panel

Click a block and open **Settings** to fine-tune it with numbers and choices instead of drag and drop:

- All blocks: exact position and size, layer (what sits in front), rotation and phone visibility (decor).
- Text: alignment and text box on/off. Button: text, where it goes and style. Image: change image, description, cropping, rounding and link. Shape: type, colour from the theme, thickness and filled/outline.
- If you click in a section (with no block selected) the panel shows the section's content width, minimum height, own grid, section theme, link name, background, edge shapes and animation. The content width is the site's, the full width, or the section's own in px: a narrow column for a long text, or a band that runs wider than the rest of the page; the background always spans the whole width.

**The element menu**: the gear on a block's toolbar opens the same settings beside the block. They stand in three areas, Content, Style and Placement, after Pinned, which holds the settings used most; in the wide menu the four share two columns, and in the narrow one they stand under each other. The pin beside a setting moves it into Pinned and back again, so it stands in one place only. A block's own editor comes first in Content, and a block with designs has its design first in Style. The search field at the top finds a setting by a word from its name or its help text: the menu then shows only what matches, with the groups that hold it open, and Esc or an empty field brings the menu back as it was. Settings has the same search over its menu.

**Designs**: every block with more than one look (Contact details, Gallery, Statistic, Collection, Countdown, Ribbon, FAQ, Timeline, Quote, Links, Basket, Button and Shape, as well as the calendar) has a **Design** row in its menu. It opens a window with a picture of each design, a search field and a live preview of your own block in the marked design, with a line about what it is; «Use this design» applies it. A block with nothing of its own to show yet (a gallery without pictures, contact details not yet typed) is previewed with sample content.

**Colours**: the colour pickers show your theme colours as dots - if you choose one of them, the field is LINKED to the theme and follows along when you change the palette in the Theme panel (linked fields are shown with a ring). If you choose freely in the area or type a hex or RGB value, the colour is unlinked, and you can make it transparent with the slider below the hues. Your most recent free colours are under **Recent**, and with the plus button by **Saved** you build your own fixed palette (up to 12; × on a dot removes it).

**Images**: in Settings you can set the focus point (which part of the image is kept when it is cropped) and adjust brightness, contrast and saturation - without the image file itself being changed.

**Site icon**: in the Theme panel you upload an image and edit it in the icon editor: drag the image to choose the crop, zoom, adjust brightness/contrast/saturation or greyscale, and **Apply** makes a square 128px icon that is shown in the browser tab. The pencil button opens the editor again; the cross button removes the icon (then the Urd mark is used).

**Backgrounds**: a section background is built from layers that can be stacked: colour, gradient (can be animated), glow, image and grain. Add, remove and sort layers in the section's Settings: each layer is a card that opens on a press with its own controls, and is dragged by its head to change the order.

**Animations**: blocks and sections can slide or fade in when visitors scroll to them (and «lift on pointer» for a hover effect). Chosen in Settings. The preview in the editor shows the end state; the animation itself plays on the published page. Visitors who have switched off animations in their system (reduced motion) get the content without animation.

**Formatting text**: click in a text block (or in the title/text of a collection entry), and the toolbar appears by the selection: text level, bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, colours and highlighting gathered behind the palette icon (theme colours, a custom colour with a full colour picker and eyedropper, highlight with the accent or a custom colour, and remove highlighting: the A with a red line across), link (its own field in the bar), alignment, lists, quote and clear formatting. The font and base size for the whole field are set in Settings. The bar closes when you click anywhere outside the field.

**The «?» help chip**: blocks with special functions (such as Collection and Calendar) show a «?» in the top left corner when you point at them. Click it for a help card that explains all the functions; the card stays until you click somewhere else.

**Editing images**: the pencil on an image block, a double-click on it, a click on a gallery picture or a collection picture, or «Open the image editor» in the menu opens the image editor, a large window with the picture at its true shape in the middle. Drag the frame over the picture to choose the part that shows, or click beside it to put it there, and drag a corner of the frame to zoom (the arrow keys move the frame); on a narrower screen the page keeps that part in view. Under the picture: the frame's shape (for an image block free, the picture's own, 1:1, 4:3, 3:2, 16:9, 9:16 or 4:5, which keeps the block's height and sets its width; for a collection picture auto, wide, 1:1, tall or round), fill or the whole picture, and the zoom. At the right: twelve looks (black and white, sepia, warm, duotone with its two colours for the shadows and the highlights, each one of the site's colours or one of your own, and more), turning a quarter left or right, mirroring, straightening a tilted horizon, brightness, contrast, saturation and blur, the corners, an overlay in one of the site's colours or one of your own, the description (read by screen readers and shown when the image cannot be loaded), and the size: the file's weight against what a picture there should weigh, with «Make smaller» when it is over, and whether the picture is sharp at the size its frame draws it. Every value beside a slider can be typed, or stepped with its small arrows or the keyboard's up and down arrows (Shift for ten steps). In a gallery, «Apply to all» gives every picture the open one's look, adjustments and overlay. Hold «Compare» to see the picture as it was, use the window's own undo and redo, reset one section or everything, and close with «Done»; Escape cancels the whole edit, and one undo in the admin takes a finished edit back. In a gallery's menu, the pencil on each picture opens the editor on that picture. Everything is drawn by the page: the picture file itself is never changed, except by «Make smaller». A picture you add is made light enough for its place as it comes in, and one that cannot be (an animation or a drawing) is asked about before it is kept. The same editor opens on the site icon (Site panel), a page's sharing image (its settings, cut to the 1200 x 630 link previews show), the logo in the Nav and Footer panels (cut to a shape if you like), the quote's portrait (in its circle; a double-click on it too), an icon you upload for a link or the icon block (as a square), a background's image layer (its look, brightness, contrast, saturation and blur, while its size and position stay in the panel) and a slideshow layer's pictures (the part each keeps in view). The site icon, the sharing image, a logo and an uploaded icon are saved as new files made from your picture, and the original is kept beside the icon, the sharing image and a logo, so editing them again starts from the original and loses no sharpness.

## Sections

The page is built from sections stacked on top of each other. Point at the border between two sections to get **+ New section**: it opens a gallery of ready-made section templates, grouped with a short description per template. All the templates are starting points: after insertion you edit the blocks freely as usual, and the colours follow your theme.

- **Basics**: empty section, hero (left-aligned or centred with two buttons), images, contact, privacy and a simple footer section.
- **Cards and lists**: feature cards with icons, news cards, events with date badges and a sign-up button, team/board, FAQ, step by step, lead story (one big story + two small) and products/merch (point the **Buy** button at a payment link, for example Vipps, in Settings).
- **Highlight**: CTA banner («Join us»), quote, statistics numbers, a greyscale sponsor row and membership with price tiers.

The toolbar in the top right of a section (shown when you point at it):

- **+ card / + row / + person …**: sections made from templates with repeating elements (feature cards, news, events, team, FAQ, steps, products, statistics, sponsors, images) have their own plus button that adds one more element, placed ready in the next free slot. The section grows when needed, and Ctrl+Z undoes the whole element in one go.
- ↑ / ↓ move the section up or down the page
- ⧉ duplicate the section: the copy lands directly below, also in the section's right-click menu
- ⤓ fit the height to the content
- ⚙ the section's settings, beside it in the same floating menu a block's gear opens (Style and Placement, with duplicate, move and delete at the foot); «Settings» in its right-click menu opens the same
- × delete the section: the first press turns it red, the second deletes, as in the right-click menu
- A named section shows its name (`#priser`) at the start of the toolbar

You can also drag the bottom edge of the section to adjust the height freely, or drag directly on the **+ New section** button on the border between two sections (a click still opens the menu; dragging moves the border). Blocks can deliberately hang over the section edge; nothing is clipped.

### Linking to a spot on a page

A link can land on a section or a block instead of the top of the page. Give the spot a name under **Link name**: for a section in its **Settings**, for a block in its menu under **Placement**, in both cases in the **Link target** group. Type the name in your own words: «Priser» is written as `priser`, and the suggestion below the field offers a name made from the spot's first heading. A name another spot on the page already has is refused with a note. To link to the spot, choose its page and then the spot in any link field (see Links below). The copy button beside the name copies the link within the site, for example `/kaker#priser`, for sharing. A spot without a name can still be linked to: the link editor lists every section by its number and first words. Under the name stand the links that point at the spot from the same page, the menu and the footer; press one to go to it.

The link lands with the spot right below the menu, from the same page and from any other. On the same page it scrolls there smoothly, unless the visitor has asked for less motion. A block hidden on phones lands on the top of its section there. A copied or duplicated block arrives without its name, and a template whose name the page already has gets a number after it (`priser-2`).

### Links

Every link in the editor is set in the same window: a button's **Goes to**, an image's or a gallery picture's link, the basket's checkout page, the calendar announcement's link, the menu's items, the footer's links and call to action, the site's links, a collection entry's link, a plugin block's link, and words in a text. Click the link field (in a text, select the words and press the link button or Ctrl+K; Ctrl+Shift+K takes the link away). Under **Goes to** choose:

- **Page**: search the pages and pick one, then, if you like, **A spot on the page**: the top, a section by its number and first words, or a block that has a link name.
- **Web address**: a full address; `www.example.org` gets `https://` in front.
- **Email**: the address, and a subject if you want one.
- **Phone**: the number.
- **File**: a file the site already has, found by searching, or a PDF from your computer with **Upload a PDF** (at most 15 MB), which is published to the media folder with the next publish. **Download instead of opening** makes the visitor's browser save it. Words in a text take a file that is already published.

**Open in a new tab** works for pages, web addresses and files, and a web address can also be marked **Ask search engines not to follow it** or **Paid or sponsored link**. Selected words that are an email address or a phone number are offered as one. A link to a page keeps working when the page's address changes or its spot is renamed. A link whose page or spot has been deleted shows a warning in its field, and so does an address within the site that no page has; click the field to choose where it goes.

## Grid (guide lines)

The **Grid** panel controls the grid that blocks snap to when you drag them. **Grid** under Show in the top bar, or the G key, shows it on every section, and it shows while a block is dragged in any case. The grid is counted out from the middle of the content: a line stands on its centre and the part cell left over is split equally between the two edges, so a block lined up with the grid can stand centred.

- **Cell size**: how tight the grid is.
- **Snap to grid**: switch it off to place completely freely. Hold Ctrl (Cmd on a Mac) while you drag to override the snapping temporarily.
- **Drawn as**: **Lines** (a quiet grid with every fourth line stronger, the default), **Dots** (a dot where the lines would cross) or **In a drag** (the grid only while a block is dragged; shown at rest, it draws the content's quarters as faint columns). Kept in your browser.
- **Guides** under Show in the top bar draws the content's quarters (25, 50 and 75 %) as thin lines with their share written at the top, or as ticks on the section's edges where the grid is drawn as dots.
- A section can have its own grid, independent of the rest of the page.

The grid is only an aid while editing: changing it never moves any content, and visitors never see it.

## Phone

Visitors on phones automatically get the content in one column, in natural reading order. You usually do not have to do anything.

- **The phone icon in the top bar** shows the page as it looks on a phone.
- If you want to fine-tune, drag the blocks in the phone view: the section then switches to **manual phone layout**, where you place everything yourself. ↺ on the section takes it back to the automatic layout.
- **Phone review**: if you change something on desktop in a section that is hand-tuned for phones, the section is marked yellow and the top bar says so («1 section needs a phone review»). That only means: take a look in the phone view and check that everything still looks good, and confirm with ✓.

## Pages, menu and theme

- The **Pages** panel: create a new page (type the name and press Enter), and give pages a new name or a new address. The gear on each row opens the page's **Page settings** (Ctrl+K reaches the open page's too): its name and address, the nav, the announcement bar and the footer, each on or off for the page (with the nav hidden, the announcement bar stands alone at the top), a search title with a preview of the title it gives, the description, hide from search engines, the sharing title, description and image, and the page's actions: **Make front page** (the page takes /, the old front page its own address, and the nav's links follow), **Save as template**, **Delete the page** and **Duplicate the page**, whose copy, «… (copy)» with its own address, opens as a draft and stays out of the menu until the Nav panel adds it. **Page settings** in Urd settings opens the settings as a dialog over the admin, a step in the panel, or a table of every page. The front page cannot be deleted; «Make front page» on another page puts that page in its place. New pages are added to the menu automatically and become visible to visitors only when you publish. The **Site** panel's **Title in the tab and in search** sets every page's title to «Page - Site», «Site - Page» or the page alone. The eye on each row, and **Hide** at the top of the page's settings, hides the page from visitors while it stays in the admin to work on: it leaves the nav, the sitemap and its address; hiding a published page asks where its visitors go meanwhile (the front page, another page, or «not found»), and showing it again takes that away. A hidden page is not private: its content file can still be fetched by anyone who knows its address.
- **Redirects** (a row under the pages in the Pages panel, and Ctrl+K) sends old addresses on to new ones: an old address from the root (`/old-page`, or `/blog/*` for everything under a folder), where it goes (a page's address or a whole web address), and **Permanent** (moved for good: browsers remember it and search engines move the page over) or **Temporary** (for now: browsers ask each time and search engines keep the old address). A rule that hides a page or leads in a circle stands in dark red, one that may not work in amber, and publishing asks before such a rule goes out. **Paste a list**, **Import CSV** and **Export CSV** move many at once. When a published page gets a new address, **Keep /old-address working** under the address is on by itself; deleting a published page asks where its visitors go. A `_redirects` file added by hand earlier is read into the list.
- The **Nav** panel: the menu at the top of the page. Change the text, choose which page (or external address) each item goes to, move an item by pressing, holding and dragging its row (or with Alt and the arrow keys), and remove it with the red cross in its open card. The logo can be text, an uploaded image or both (with size and order), and always works as a «Home» button. The menu items can sit to the right, centred or to the left. An item can carry a one-line description (shown in the submenus and the phone menu) and be shown as a button; a submenu item can have items of its own, down to three submenu levels: each opens beside the panel or stands as a headed group when the submenu has columns or is the flyout. In the Nav panel an item is dragged onto the middle of another to go under it, onto its top or bottom to stand beside it. A right click on the menu in the page opens its own menu: «Settings» opens the settings of the part under the pointer (the menu items, the logo, the burger under Phone, the announcement, and the link launcher, the cart, the theme button and the links each in its own card under Tools), and each of the Nav panel's folds stands below it. Under Tools the tools stand as cards in their order in the menu, dragged to a new place, and the link launcher's shortcuts are cards of their own. Under Phone you choose the burger's drawing and whether the word «Menu» stands beside it, and under «Show nav on pages» you keep the menu off chosen pages, where it can stay hidden, slide in when the pointer reaches the top edge, show as a ghost there, or hang as a pull tab in the default look or your own. Under Colours and frame you set the menu's text colours, its background in layers and its border; «Sticky menu» (whether the menu follows along when visitors scroll down) and «Transparent at the top of the page» stand under Scrolling and pages.
- **Site icon**: at the bottom of the Theme panel you upload the icon that is shown in the browser tab and bookmarks (a square image is recommended).
- The **Collections** panel: lists of entries (news, notices, publications) that live as data and are shown by Collection blocks. Create a collection, write entries (title, date, text, image, link), and put a Collection block on the page (or use the section templates «News (collection)», «Noticeboard» and «Publication archive»). Adding a news item is then to WRITE an entry - all the views follow automatically. The block's Settings choose collection, view (cards/list/archive per year), count and sorting. The panel steps in from the collections to a collection's entries, with a search, and on to one entry, with arrows to the entry before or after; «Collections editor» in Urd settings shows a collection as two columns in a widened panel, or as a table over the page with the entry in a drawer. Drag an entry's row to move it, or press Alt and the up or down arrow key on it (an entry with a date stands by its date). Select entries with the box that stands in place of the drag grip (Shift takes a run, Ctrl+A every entry shown); while some are selected, a press on a row selects or deselects it. The bar under the search duplicates or deletes the selected, and the collection's ⋮ menu sets a field on all of them, moves or copies them to another collection, exports them or deletes them, its selection items greyed while none is selected; each step is undone with «Undo» in the status line. The collection's ⋮ menu renames it, changes its kind where another kind fits its entries (a products collection keeps its own), and holds the CSV out and in. The export opens straight in a spreadsheet, in its columns and with its letters, and a re-imported file shows what it will do before anything is stored: which rows are new, which change an entry (each change old to new), which are not stored and why, and which entries the file does not name. Rows that match an entry can update it or be skipped, and entries the file does not name can stay or go; a column the file lacks leaves its field as it is. **Import preview** in Urd settings shows this as a dialog, a step in the panel or a table with every cell, and **Show every cell** opens the table from the other two. An entry's picture opens the same image editor as a picture on the page.
- The **Plugins** panel: extensions that give Urd new blocks and section templates. The panel shows the plugins in the repo's plugins/ folder; switch them off and on with the toggle, and publish as usual. Active plugins work immediately in the preview (visitors get them after publishing), and the plugin's blocks appear in the **+ New block** menu in the sections. The one plugin that ships is the Swedish language pack; calendar, form and map are core blocks (see below) and need no plugin.

  The Calendar block shows a calendar you keep in your own calendar app. It is in the Blocks panel and the **+ New block** menu under **Events and places**: **Calendar**, and **All calendar designs …**, which opens a picture of each of the 34 designs; or use the section template «What is on». Select the block and paste the calendar's iCal address or Google calendar id under **Sources** in its menu (a row per calendar); a source can be given a name and a colour, and the name becomes the events' category. In the same menu you choose the design, the count, and what visitors get: a calendar filter, a search field, earlier events, a view switcher, and the **Subscribe** and **Sign up** buttons. A press on an event opens its card with the whole description, a map link, and **Join** for a video meeting. The calendar has a guide of its own: [The calendar](../calendar-guide/CALENDAR-en-GB.md) (calendar services, designs, meeting links, sign-up, maps and more).

  The Form block (and the «Contact form» template) is in the Blocks panel: select the block, and set the recipient, fields and send mode in Settings. By default the form opens the visitor's email client with a ready-made email (no setup). If you would rather send to your own endpoint (Apps Script or a function of your own), choose «External endpoint» and paste the address; then you must open connect-src for that host in _headers (the block explains the line). The fields can be added, changed and removed.

  The Map block (and the «Find us» template) is in the Blocks panel: select the block and, in the «Location» field in Settings, type an address (e.g. «1 High Street, Anytown»), coordinates (such as «59.913, 10.739») or paste an OpenStreetMap link, and set «Zoom» and «Height». The address search looks up the place via OpenStreetMap when you click «Search». The map is OpenStreetMap's own embed without tracking, and Urd's standard _headers allows it, so it works out of the box. (On another host «frame-src https://www.openstreetmap.org» must be in _headers; the block says so if the map is blocked.)
- The **Legal** panel gathers what an association must get right, in three groups: **Who you are** (the contact details below), **Privacy** (the privacy page the footer links) and **Terms of sale** (the terms page the checkout links, shown while the site sells, the shop is on or a terms page is chosen). Where Cloudflare has put its own visit counting on the published pages (it does so by itself for a domain on its free plan), Privacy says **Cloudflare counts the visits**, the privacy page tells it, and the «?» beside it says how to turn it off in Cloudflare. Under Privacy, **How long you keep** sets, for the kinds the site has, how long the association keeps form messages (a set time after each arrives, or until the matter is closed) the newsletter list (until the reader unsubscribes, or a set time) and, while the site has a checkout, orders: **We keep accounts by law** (five years after the end of the financial year under bokføringsloven § 13, the sales record kept apart), **A sports club under NIF** (the same five years under NIFs lov § 2-11) or a set time, the two duties offered for an association in Norway, and the «?» beside Orders naming the thresholds that put an association under each; the privacy page says each in every design, the order's law linked, and one left at **Not decided** is said to be not decided yet, with the association's email to ask. Each group's heading says what is filled in or missing, and an amber dot on the panel's tab marks that something the law asks for is missing: the legal name or the email while the site has a form or a shop, a privacy page while it has a form, or parts of the terms page while it sells; the group headings say which. Where no privacy or terms page is chosen, **Make the privacy page** and **Make the terms of sale page** open the Pages panel with the template picked. In the strip and the panel's tabs, the menu under **Legal** marks the groups that need looking at with the same dot. The Site panel's **Contact details** row, the Footer panel's **Privacy page** and the Shop group's **Terms of sale page** show what is chosen and open the Legal panel at it.
- The **Legal** panel's **Who you are**: the association's country, legal name, organisation numbers, address, phone and email, typed once. The country decides which numbers there are (57 countries are known; «Other country» gives a free number with its own name), how each is written and checked, and how the VAT number is formed. The footer shows the legal line at the bottom («Apeiron Linjeforening · Org.nr. 974 760 673 MVA») and the address, phone and email as a contact column, and search engines read the same details. A number that fails its check is marked and not saved; where a register issues numbers that fail the usual check, it is saved with a note. An empty field leaves no trace. The **Contact details** block (and the «Contact» section template) shows the same details on a page, so a changed phone number changes everywhere; in its menu you choose which parts show and, under Style, one of eleven designs: Lines, Icon rows, Card, Three tiles, Fact list, Action buttons, With a map, Contact strip, Poster, By role and With a QR code. Each has its own layout on a phone. «With a map» shows the address on OpenStreetMap, loaded only when the visitor taps «Show map»; click «Find on the map» under the address in the Legal panel first (the lookup goes through the published site). «By role» lists the **Contact points** you add in the Legal panel (the board, the treasurer, the press, each with an email, a phone and a note). «With a QR code» draws a code that saves the contact on another phone. Two options work with the designs: an «Add to contacts» button that downloads the details as a contact card, and a call bar on phones that keeps Call, Email and Map at the bottom of the screen while the visitor scrolls (one bar per page).
- The **Updates** panel's **Media** group: everything you upload is stored under `media/` in a folder for its kind (`images/`, `files/`, `audio/`, `video/`). A site built before the folders came has its files at the root of `media/`; the group counts them, and **Tidy the media folder** moves them into their folders and updates every page, menu, footer and collection that uses them, as one publish. Nothing is uploaded again, and a site that never presses it works as before.
- The **Footer** panel: the footer shown at the bottom of all pages. Switch it on under **Show on pages** and fill in its parts - it is edited in one place and applies to the whole site. Its parts stand in folds the Footer tab's menu reaches: where it shows, its design and starting point, its look and background, the brand, the columns (cards that open on a press, each with its own links), **Links and contact**, the call to action and the **Bottom row**. Under **Links and contact** two switches leave the legal line, or the address, phone and email, out of the footer; both are on to begin with, since most countries want them easy to find.
- The **Theme** panel: the colours and fonts the whole site builds on. If you change the accent colour, buttons, links and highlights follow along everywhere.

## The shop

A shop is three blocks: **Product cards** show a product collection, the **Basket** holds what the visitor has picked, and the **Checkout** takes the order, as an email to you or to an order endpoint you have set up, or as a payment through a provider: Vipps (or MobilePay), a card on Vipps's or Stripe's page, or PayPal. Each provider is switched on under **Payment** in the Checkout's menu once its keys are in place; the setup guide walks you through each, and the **Shop currency** in the Site panel is the currency they charge in.

A product's **Presets**, beside its sizes, add a set of sizes in one press: the sets your other products use, common sets, or numbers in your own wording, where # stands for the number («#-pack» gives «2-pack, 4-pack, 6-pack»). A size changes no price yet. Turn on **The buyer types the quantity** to put a quantity field on the card, with a least and a most that may each stay empty: the price is for one, and the basket and the payment keep to the limits.

The checkout follows the EU consumer rights directive (2011/83/EU), which applies in the whole EEA; the sections named below are in Norway's angrerettloven, which implements it. In another EEA country the same rules stand in that country's own act. Outside the EEA, check your country's rules on distance selling: the button's words, the confirmation and the withdrawal form follow the EEA's.

- The order button reads «Confirm purchase», and the order lines with the total stand right above the buttons. The EU consumer rights rules ask for both; in Norway that is angrerettloven § 16. The button's words weigh most: a button that only says «Send» or «Order» does not say that ordering means paying, and then the buyer is not bound by the order. That is why the text cannot be changed.
- The total above the buttons has no shipping line yet; the shop gets one in v1.1. If you charge for shipping, put it in the product prices, since the same rules ask for the delivery cost before the order is placed.
- With the email draft, the buyer places the order by sending the email from their own mail program.
- Under the buttons the Checkout names the seller: the legal name, organisation number, address, phone and email from the Legal panel's Who you are, since the buyer must know who they buy from before the order. «Show the seller» in the block's menu switches it off.
- **Terms of sale**: a new page from the **Terms of sale** template holds what the buyer must be told before the order (in Norway angrerettloven § 8): the seller, prices, ordering, payment, delivery, the right of withdrawal with the EEA's standard withdrawal form, defects and complaints, personal data and disputes. It is written in the site's language with your details from the Legal panel and a link to the privacy page; the parts in [brackets] are yours to fill in, since only you know how you ship and take payment. They are marked on the page in the editor until you write over them (visitors never see the mark), the page's row in the Pages panel counts those left, and publishing the page with any left asks you first. The page starts in the **Long read** design, with the main points as a short list under the introduction (drawn as cards in **At a glance**) and the withdrawal form as a card a visitor can print alone. Every Checkout then links the page under its buttons; **Terms of sale page** under Terms of sale in the **Legal** panel chooses another page, or none.
- «Pay with Vipps», «Pay with card» and «Pay with PayPal» name the payment itself, a wording the EU Commission's guidance on the rule accepts.
- With a provider the buyer can leave the contact fields empty: the provider's page asks for them, and the Checkout thanks the buyer by name when they come back. The money stays with the provider until you take it: Vipps holds it until you capture it in the Vipps portal when the goods ship; Stripe holds it or takes it at once, as **Take the money** says; PayPal takes it at once.
- **The order confirmation** is your duty as the seller: the buyer must have the agreement confirmed on a durable medium by delivery at the latest (in Norway angrerettloven § 18). After every order, by email, by endpoint or through a provider, the Checkout shows a confirmation with the order lines, the total, the buyer, how it is paid, your name, organisation number and contact details, and the EEA's standard withdrawal form, with a **Print** button that prints it alone. It stays on the page until the next order in that tab. The email draft carries your details too, so the buyer's sent mail holds the same.
- **Payment instructions** are ways to pay without a provider, such as a Vipps number, a bank account or paying at pickup: a name and a text each. One stands as a line above the order; with two or more the buyer picks one before confirming, the order email or the endpoint names it, and the thank-you after the order repeats it.

## Drafts and publishing

- Everything you do is saved as a draft in your browser, also across pages. While something is unpublished, **Publish** carries a count: each page with changes, the site settings, a collection, the templates, the plugins and the redirects count one each.
- **Publish** opens its menu, on a press or when the mouse rests on it: **Publish now** puts all the drafts out on the site (it takes about one minute before visitors see them), **View the published site** opens the live page in a new tab, and **Discard drafts** deletes the drafts and takes you back to the site as it is published, after asking. Under them, **Not published yet** lists the changes; a press on one takes you to it. The foot names the last publish and opens **History**.
- The **History** panel shows the same list under **Not published yet**, with **Discard drafts** at its foot (it is also at the foot of the undo buttons' list). The message after a discard brings the drafts back while nothing else has been done.

Publishing requires that your GitHub user has been given access by the site owner (see [the setup guide](../setup-publication/SETUP-en-GB.md)).

If several of you are editing, the editor says so if someone else has published changes in the same parts since you loaded the page, and lets you choose whether to publish anyway or look at the changes first.

## History and undoing a publish

The **History** panel shows what waits to be published first, under **Not published yet**, and below it the most recent publishes: what was changed, by whom and when. **↩ Undo last publish** rolls the site back to how it was before the previous publish. The undo is itself a publish, so nothing is deleted from the history, and you can undo the undo. After an undo you reload admin (after ~1 minute) before you edit further; the editor says so.

(Ctrl+Z undoes drafts in the browser BEFORE you publish; the History panel undoes what is already published.)

## The first time

If you open admin on a completely fresh site, you get a short wizard: the site's name and two colours. Everything it sets can be changed later in the Theme and Nav panels.
